**Make the Deepavali flower feel luminous**

The reference calls for three coordinated effects: **bright, selective emission on the decorative lines; bloom around those lines; and colored light on the surrounding geometry**. Your current scene already has camera HDR and post-processing enabled, so a missing camera switch is not the main problem. The active bloom profile is intentionally subtle, some ornament channels emit no light, and the scene has no working emissive GI or palette-colored spill lights. My recommended first experiment is stronger, better-resolved bloom plus three or four palette-driven realtime lights, while keeping the petal interiors relatively dark. This is a proposed tuning baseline; no scene settings or assets were changed during the research.

Intensity controls bloom strength, while scatter controls its spread. Half resolution and high-quality filtering are sensible trials for the thin contours and tiny dots in this artwork. Keep the bloom tint white so different emitters produce their own colored halos. Clamp limits the brightness used to calculate bloom; raising it only helps when the source exceeds that limit. If later emission peaks exceed 8, compare a clamp of 32. Avoid lowering the threshold close to zero: ordinary lit surfaces can begin contributing haze. ([Unity Bloom](https://docs.unity.com/en-us/engine/6000.0/manual/post-processing-and-full-screen-effects/urp/effect-list/bloom), [installed URP Bloom implementation](<F:/Freelance_Work/amgen-LEDWall/Amgen Interactive Wall/Library/PackageCache/com.unity.render-pipelines.universal@dd24be0d7cf1/Runtime/Overrides/Bloom.cs:42>))

For illumination on the surroundings, **add actual lights synchronized with the palette transition**. The current scene has a warm near-white directional light and a weak cool point fill, with no palette-colored local lights. Realtime GI is disabled, there are no baked lightmaps, and petal materials are marked `EmissiveIsBlack` for GI contribution. Start with **three or four broad, shadowless point lights**, distributed around the rangoli slightly above the floor. Blend their color with the existing seven-state palette, and give spill intensity a separate control from ornament emission. Set their ranges to reach nearby decorations rather than the whole frame. Keep diya lights warm through every state. Your ground already uses URP Lit and can receive these lights; adding subtle surface and normal texture would make the illumination easier to perceive. A soft, tinted floor-halo mesh can supplement the lights for a broader pool, but that artistic layer will not illuminate raised decorations.

Baked emissive GI is useful for a fixed look, but one bake will not automatically follow seven changing palettes. Unity 6.0 does support configured Enlighten realtime GI in URP; it requires scene preparation and updates indirect lighting over multiple frames. That is a larger investment than this effect needs initially. The existing Forward+ renderer already supports the proposed small light rig. ([Unity Enlighten guidance](https://docs.unity3d.com/6000.0/Documentation/Manual/realtime-gi-using-enlighten.html), [URP Forward+](https://docs.unity3d.com/6000.0/Documentation/Manual/urp/rendering/forward-rendering-paths.html))

Leave Post Exposure near zero while balancing emission, bloom, and lighting. In this URP implementation, it runs **after bloom**, so increasing exposure does not make additional source pixels qualify for bloom; it brightens the processed image. Compare Neutral tonemapping first because it compresses highlights with relatively little hue and saturation change. ACES is another artistic option, but check the blue and orange states carefully. ([Unity Color Adjustments](https://docs.unity.com/en-us/engine/6000.0/manual/post-processing-and-full-screen-effects/urp/effect-list/color-adjustments), [Unity Tonemapping](https://docs.unity.com/en-us/engine/6000.0/manual/post-processing-and-full-screen-effects/urp/effect-list/tonemapping))

Judge the result at the LED wall's actual resolution and viewing distance: the contours should retain a sharp bright core, the halo should remain visibly colored, and nearby surfaces should show matching illumination. Internal HDR rendering can produce this look on SDR output; enabling HDR display output is a separate hardware decision.
